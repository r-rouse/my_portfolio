# Bio

Hello, I’m Randall. This is my website.

It’s mostly for work, but since it’s pretty hard to stand out in the AI-slop job market, I figured I’d be as authentic as I can be.

I’m currently looking for work in the Pacific Northwest. My beautiful partner took a role at Nike, so we’re heading to Oregon. I love Oregon, so I’m very happy about the move.

My road here has been a little winding.

I’ll start back in 2015 and try to be brief.

I moved from Tuscaloosa, Alabama, to Denver with $800 and a dream. I wanted to go to college, and I wanted to be somewhere new and fun. I worked odd jobs and in restaurants until I qualified for in-state tuition, then started university studying a lifelong interest of mine: biology, with a focus on ecology and botany.

I worked full-time in hospitality while going to school full-time. I TA’d for my favorite biology professor, helped pilot a field-based ecological restoration course for my university, and took an internship at CU Boulder that showed me there was actually a path toward becoming a career scientist.

In December 2019, I became the first person in my family to graduate from college. I was stoked for the future.

Then COVID happened.

Oh no.

Everything shut down just as I was graduating and the world flipped upside down. I started applying for jobs anyway and landed a spot on a wildland firefighting crew out of Denver.

I loved every minute of it.

We traveled around the West and Midwest doing prescribed burns and wildfire work. I dug line, ran chainsaws, got very dirty, worked around helicopters, and spent plenty of nights cowboy camping. It was hard work, but I really enjoyed that life.

At the end of the season, my partner took a job in Boston—which, unsurprisingly, doesn’t have a huge wildland firefighting industry.

So I packed my bags and headed east.

I’d managed to save some money while working in the woods because, well, there aren’t many places to spend money in the woods. I started thinking about what I wanted to do for my next act and decided I wanted to learn how to code.

It had always bothered me that I didn’t really understand how the digital world around me worked. I depended on software every day, I’d always been a tinkerer, and I wanted to understand what was actually happening underneath all of it.

So I used my savings to attend a coding bootcamp.

It was possibly harder than firefighting, but I learned to code.

Since then, I’ve worked professionally as a software engineer—first building a mobile application, then working on a large business-intelligence SaaS platform, and now working on agentic AI integration.

It’s been a strange path: restaurants → ecology → wildland fire → software → AI.

But I don’t really see those things as disconnected. I like learning how complicated systems work. I like solving problems. I like building things. And I like work that has some tangible relationship to the world outside of a computer screen.

If I could design my dream next chapter, I’d use the technical skills I’ve developed over the last several years in a way that reconnects me with conservation, public service, or civic-minded work.

Anyhow, that’s my story.

I hope we can connect—and maybe work together.

— Randall
